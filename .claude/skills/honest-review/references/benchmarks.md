# Benchmark Sheet

What each benchmark does best, so a reviewer compares against a *specific*
feature, not a vague memory of "a good game". A score of 8+ must name one row
here (or an equally specific feature) and say how the reviewed game compares.

These are well-known, stable design facts about shipped games. A reviewer who is
unsure of a detail writes "benchmark detail unverified" instead of inventing one.

## Dead Cells (Motion Twin / Evil Empire, 2018)
- **Feel:** near-instant input response, animation cancels, generous dodge
  i-frames, hit-stop and knockback on every hit — the genre reference for C1, C2, C6.
- **Routes:** branching biome paths; the player chooses which biome comes next (R1).
- **Builds:** colour-scaling stats (Brutality / Tactics / Survival) steer builds;
  two weapons plus two skills; mutations add passives (S1, S3).
- **Enemy design:** each enemy has one clear question and a readable tell (C4).
- **Difficulty:** Boss Cells raise difficulty in stages and add content (B5).
- **Content at launch:** ~15+ biomes over time, 100+ weapons/skills; daily
  challenge with a leaderboard (R3, R6).
- **Meta:** permanent unlocks of blueprints with Cells; the rune abilities open
  new routes (Metroidvania gating) (R2).

## Skul: The Hero Slayer (SouthPAW Games, 2021)
- **Identity swap:** skulls change moveset, range and playstyle mid-run, swap
  with a cooldown and a swap attack — the reference for S4 and C7.
- **Rarity tiers** for skulls and items; inscriptions (set bonuses) form synergies (S2).
- **Bosses:** large, multi-phase, spectacular set pieces with a phase transition (C5).
- **Inverted fantasy:** you are the demon-side underdog; tone is light (N3, I2).
- **Pixel art cohesion:** one consistent pixel style across characters and FX (V2).

## Rogue Legacy 2 (Cellar Door Games, 2022)
- **Lineage:** each death passes to an heir with random traits that change
  play (positive and negative) — the reference for S5, N5.
- **Classes:** 15 classes with distinct weapons and talents (S5, C7).
- **Manor/castle:** a persistent upgrade tree as the main gold sink (B4, R2).
- **House Rules:** granular difficulty and accessibility sliders (B5, U4).
- **Biomes with heirlooms:** permanent traversal abilities gate areas (R3).
- **Scars / NG+ Burdens:** challenge layers for the late game (R3).

## Hades (Supergiant Games, 2020)
- **Story in the loop:** every death returns you to the House where the story
  advances; thousands of reactive lines (N1, N2, R4).
- **Boons:** Olympian gods offer themed boons; Duo boons combine two gods;
  each god has a visual and sound identity (S1, S2, S6, I1).
- **Weapons and aspects:** 6 weapons x 4 aspects each change play (C7, S5).
- **Heat (Pact of Punishment):** modular difficulty with rewards (B5).
- **God Mode:** an accessibility mode that scales damage resistance per death (U4).
- **Music:** Darren Korb's score; boss songs with vocals; music defines tone (A1, A2).
- **Art:** Jen Zee's illustrated character art, consistent across portraits,
  sprites and UI (V1, V2).
- **Room rewards shown on doors:** the player picks the next reward (S3, R1).
- **Run length:** ~25-45 minutes, four biomes, a boss at each end (R5).

## Using the sheet

- Compare on the **criterion**, not on the whole game: the reviewed game may
  beat Skul on narrative and lose to it on identity swap.
- "Our game has X like Hades" is not a 9. A 9 needs X to *work as well* as in
  Hades, shown by evidence.
- Volume comparisons (R6) count shipped content in the build, not planned content.
